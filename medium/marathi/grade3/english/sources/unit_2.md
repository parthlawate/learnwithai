## 2.2 Narali Pournima

*(Book Page 27)*

### a. Listen, read and discuss.

Mukta and Raghav are siblings. They live in a small village near Alibaug. Alibaug is famous for its long seashore and coconut trees. Their father is a fisherman. Their mother makes tasty coconut sweets. Also, she makes *solkadhi* by using coconut flesh.

They use coconut shells to decorate their house. They make doormats from brown husk.

Today, Mukta woke up early. She said, “Today is a very special day.” Raghav asked “What's special Mukta?”

She smiled and said, “Today is **Narali Pournima** and **Rakshabandhan**.” Their mother called them to have some ‘*Narali Bhat*’ for breakfast.

In the evening, whole family reached the seashore. Her mother prepared a *pooja thali* to worship the sea. At the end they offered a coconut to the sea.

They spent the whole day with their relatives. They enjoyed sweets like *Narali Bhat*, coconut *laddoos*, *barfi*, etc.

> **Coconut Tree says:**  
> "Hello! You can make different craft articles from my shell. Enjoy doing it."  
> *(Articles shown: small drums, owl, rabbit made of coconut shell)*

### b. Listen and read.

When I was very small, I often looked at the clock on the wall. It made a sound again and again, “tick-tick, tick-tick.”

At that time, I thought the clock was only making noise. I did not know that it was trying to tell me something.

As I grew a little older, I began to notice its numbers and hands. The big hand and the small hand moved round and round. Slowly, I understood that the clock had a special language. Always it showed me the time. The clock became my good friend.

Even now, it tells me when to get up in the morning. It reminds me when to go to school. It shows me the time to study and do my homework. It shows me time to play with my friends.

When the Sun sets, the clock reminds me to eat my dinner. It tells me that it is time to go to bed. In this way, the clock helps me every day. It keeps me healthy and active.

### b. Listen and sing.

A **Capital Letter** stands up tall,  
It starts the sentence, big and small.  

A **Comma** takes a tiny pause,  
It rests our breath and helps the clause.

This is the finish, my little friend.”  

A **Question Mark** bends its head,  
It asks, “Who? What? Why?” instead.  

Together they make reading bright,
